About the Fellowship

Bridging the gap between academic research and urgent state-level decisions across Maine.

Why Maine Needs Embedded Science

State lawmakers and agency leaders navigate increasingly technical decisions—from working waterfront resilience and offshore wind transmission to affordable, climate-ready housing and rural STEM workforce pipelines. Yet state governments rarely have dedicated in-house scientific staff to synthesize emerging evidence on legislative timelines.

Our Host: Maine Sea Grant

Anchored at Maine Sea Grant, the fellowship draws on decades of trusted, non-advocacy coastal extension and stakeholder engagement. Fellows operate as objective technical resources, translating peer-reviewed data into accessible briefings while gaining firsthand experience in public governance.

Core Research & Policy Focus Areas

Fellows support legislative committees and executive agencies across four interconnected domains.

Coastal Science

Sea-level rise adaptation, working waterfront infrastructure, marine ecosystem health, and sustainable fisheries management.

Energy

Offshore wind integration, grid modernization, beneficial electrification, and equitable rural energy transitions.

Housing

Climate-resilient building standards, municipal land-use planning, and sustainable housing supply for Maine communities.

Education

STEM career pathways, marine and climate literacy, and strengthening connections between Maine universities and public schools.

Advisory Board

Guided by leaders at the intersection of Maine science, marine policy, and public governance.

Advisory Board Member

David Reidmiller, PhD

Chief Climate Officer & Director of the Climate Center, Gulf of Maine Research Institute (GMRI)

Dr. David Reidmiller leads climate science and solutions initiatives at the Gulf of Maine Research Institute (GMRI), working with coastal communities, industries, and policymakers to navigate rapid environmental change in the Gulf of Maine. Prior to joining GMRI, he served as Director of the Northeast and Southeast Climate Adaptation Science Centers at the U.S. Geological Survey, led the federal Fifth National Climate Assessment (NCA5) team at the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy (OSTP), and served as a senior science advisor at the U.S. Department of State. On the Advisory Board, Dr. Reidmiller provides strategic guidance on connecting rigorous scientific research with actionable state policy.
